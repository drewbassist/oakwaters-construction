Choosing the Right Flooring for Each Room

Match flooring to moisture exposure, traffic, comfort, and maintenance.

Retrospective topic: March 2025 · Oakwaters Journal

A successful choosing the right flooring for each room project begins with decisions that affect how the home works, not just how it looks. For homeowners in Ontario, the best results come from checking existing conditions, coordinating the relevant trades and making selections before work reaches the point where changes become expensive.

Match flooring to moisture exposure

Entryways, bathrooms and basements see different moisture conditions. Ask whether a product is approved for the substrate and room. Water-resistant marketing language does not mean every floor is waterproof in an actual assembly.

Before requesting prices, ask the contractor to explain how this part of the work will be assessed and what assumptions the estimate makes. Request photographs or a sketch when an important condition will be hidden after installation. A clear record helps distinguish an agreed scope from an unforeseen condition.

Inspect the subfloor

Unevenness, deflection and moisture can cause premature failure. Confirm preparation requirements before selecting thin or rigid materials. Floor height changes also affect doors, stairs and transitions.

Compare at least two realistic approaches using the same criteria: installation requirements, durability, maintenance and impact on adjacent work. A lower material price may not mean a lower installed cost. Ask which decisions must be finalized before products can be ordered.

Consider sound and comfort

Hard surfaces can amplify footsteps, while underlayments have assembly-specific performance. For upper floors, consider sound transmission to rooms below. Radiant heat compatibility must be checked with the manufacturer.

Walk through the proposed layout at full scale if possible. Tape dimensions on the floor or wall, check the position of existing services and test everyday movement. This simple exercise often reveals conflicts that are difficult to notice in a small drawing.

Compare maintenance and repair

Wood can sometimes be refinished; tile may require individual replacement; resilient planks vary in repairability. Test samples for scratches and cleaning needs. Keep spare material from the same production lot when possible.

Confirm the sequence with the people doing the work. One trade may need to complete concealed preparation before another can install visible finishes. Record who supplies each item and who checks dimensions, because missing handoffs can cause delays and rework.

Plan the layout and transitions

Board direction, tile pattern, grout joints and thresholds influence the finished appearance. Resolve transitions at stairs and cabinets before installation. Avoid awkward slivers at prominent doorways.

At the final walkthrough, compare the completed work with the approved scope and product specifications. Ask for operating instructions, maintenance guidance and warranty documents where applicable. Photograph finished details and retain invoices and approvals for future reference.

Questions to ask before work begins

  • Which existing conditions must be inspected before we commit to the choosing the right flooring for each room scope?
  • Which selections and dimensions must be confirmed before ordering?
  • What work is excluded from the estimate, and how will changes be approved?
  • Are any municipal permits, inspections or licensed trades required for this specific scope?
  • How will completed work be inspected and documented?
