Choosing Interior Doors and Trim

Use small architectural details to create a consistent interior language.

Retrospective topic: May 2026 · Oakwaters Journal

A successful choosing interior doors and trim project begins with decisions that affect how the home works, not just how it looks. For homeowners in Ontario, the best results come from checking existing conditions, coordinating the relevant trades and making selections before work reaches the point where changes become expensive.

Start with the door schedule

List every opening, its width, swing direction, privacy needs and existing jamb condition. Bedroom and bathroom doors need different acoustic and privacy considerations than pantry doors. Check whether a door will collide with furniture, cabinetry or another door before choosing a style.

Before requesting prices, ask the contractor to explain how this part of the work will be assessed and what assumptions the estimate makes. Request photographs or a sketch when an important condition will be hidden after installation. A clear record helps distinguish an agreed scope from an unforeseen condition.

Choose the construction, not just the panel

Solid-core doors usually feel more substantial and limit sound transmission better than hollow-core doors, but they weigh more and need suitable hardware. For bathrooms, select finishes that tolerate humidity. Pocket doors can recover floor area, but their wall cavities cannot simply be filled with plumbing or switches.

Compare at least two realistic approaches using the same criteria: installation requirements, durability, maintenance and impact on adjacent work. A lower material price may not mean a lower installed cost. Ask which decisions must be finalized before products can be ordered.

Match trim to the architecture

Compare casing width, baseboard height, reveals and crown moulding against ceiling height and existing features. A crisp contemporary profile can look disconnected beside ornate original millwork. Mock up a full door corner with casing and baseboard before ordering materials.

Walk through the proposed layout at full scale if possible. Tape dimensions on the floor or wall, check the position of existing services and test everyday movement. This simple exercise often reveals conflicts that are difficult to notice in a small drawing.

Plan transitions and hardware

Decide where new trim meets older trim, tile, stairs and built-ins. Specify hinges, latch finishes, door stops and lever clearance together. If floors will be raised, verify finished door clearances and whether jambs need extension.

Confirm the sequence with the people doing the work. One trade may need to complete concealed preparation before another can install visible finishes. Record who supplies each item and who checks dimensions, because missing handoffs can cause delays and rework.

Check installation quality

Inspect consistent gaps, square frames, smooth latching and clean mitres. Ask the installer to address bowed walls rather than hiding every irregularity with caulk. Paint samples should be viewed under actual room lighting.

At the final walkthrough, compare the completed work with the approved scope and product specifications. Ask for operating instructions, maintenance guidance and warranty documents where applicable. Photograph finished details and retain invoices and approvals for future reference.

Questions to ask before work begins

  • Which existing conditions must be inspected before we commit to the choosing interior doors and trim scope?
  • Which selections and dimensions must be confirmed before ordering?
  • What work is excluded from the estimate, and how will changes be approved?
  • Are any municipal permits, inspections or licensed trades required for this specific scope?
  • How will completed work be inspected and documented?
