A Practical Guide to Renovation Budgets

Build a budget around scope, allowances, and uncertainty.

Retrospective topic: June 2024 · Oakwaters Journal

A successful a practical guide to renovation budgets project begins with decisions that affect how the home works, not just how it looks. For homeowners in Ontario, the best results come from checking existing conditions, coordinating the relevant trades and making selections before work reaches the point where changes become expensive.

Define scope before estimating

List the rooms, structural changes, systems and finish expectations. An estimate for cosmetic updates cannot be compared with one including plumbing relocation and insulation. Clear scope is the foundation of a meaningful budget.

Before requesting prices, ask the contractor to explain how this part of the work will be assessed and what assumptions the estimate makes. Request photographs or a sketch when an important condition will be hidden after installation. A clear record helps distinguish an agreed scope from an unforeseen condition.

Separate fixed costs and allowances

Labour, demolition and known materials may be priced differently from fixtures and finishes not yet selected. Ask what each allowance includes. A low allowance can make a proposal look cheaper while shifting cost into later selections.

Compare at least two realistic approaches using the same criteria: installation requirements, durability, maintenance and impact on adjacent work. A lower material price may not mean a lower installed cost. Ask which decisions must be finalized before products can be ordered.

Plan for hidden conditions

Opening walls can reveal water damage, outdated wiring or framing problems. Set aside a contingency appropriate to the age and uncertainty of the home, rather than spending the entire budget on finishes. The right amount depends on project risk.

Walk through the proposed layout at full scale if possible. Tape dimensions on the floor or wall, check the position of existing services and test everyday movement. This simple exercise often reveals conflicts that are difficult to notice in a small drawing.

Understand change orders

Ask how additional work is identified, priced and approved. Require a written description of cost and schedule impact before proceeding when practical. Keep a running total of approved changes.

Confirm the sequence with the people doing the work. One trade may need to complete concealed preparation before another can install visible finishes. Record who supplies each item and who checks dimensions, because missing handoffs can cause delays and rework.

Budget for the household impact

Temporary accommodation, storage, takeout meals and utility interruptions can have real costs. Include permit and design fees where applicable. Review the budget at milestones, not only at the end.

At the final walkthrough, compare the completed work with the approved scope and product specifications. Ask for operating instructions, maintenance guidance and warranty documents where applicable. Photograph finished details and retain invoices and approvals for future reference.

Questions to ask before work begins

  • Which existing conditions must be inspected before we commit to the a practical guide to renovation budgets scope?
  • Which selections and dimensions must be confirmed before ordering?
  • What work is excluded from the estimate, and how will changes be approved?
  • Are any municipal permits, inspections or licensed trades required for this specific scope?
  • How will completed work be inspected and documented?
