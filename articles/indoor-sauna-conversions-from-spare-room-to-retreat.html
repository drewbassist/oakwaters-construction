Indoor Sauna Conversions: From Spare Room to Retreat

Converting existing interior space responsibly.

Oakwaters Construction · Southern Ontario guide

A basement corner or unused room may look convenient, but a sauna is a specialized enclosure. Existing walls, insulation, ventilation paths and adjacent finishes need to be assessed before framing or purchasing a heater.

Plan the construction details

The enclosure must manage heat and moisture without trapping water vapour in vulnerable assemblies. Coordinate a suitable vapour-control strategy, ventilation, floor drainage where appropriate, and service access with the chosen sauna system.

Think about long-term use

Confirm electrical capacity, required dedicated circuits and clearances with qualified trades. A comfortable layout includes bench height, door swing, safe circulation and a dry transition area rather than squeezing the largest possible cabin into the room.

Before starting work

Discuss the intended use, site conditions, materials, construction sequence and maintenance expectations with the project team. Verify permits and applicable requirements with the local municipality and qualified trades before ordering materials or beginning work. The best result comes from resolving practical details while the design is still flexible.
