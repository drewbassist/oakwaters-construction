How to Compare Renovation Proposals

Look beyond the total to understand what is actually being offered.

Retrospective topic: August 2024 · Oakwaters Journal

A successful how to compare renovation proposals project begins with decisions that affect how the home works, not just how it looks. For homeowners in Ontario, the best results come from checking existing conditions, coordinating the relevant trades and making selections before work reaches the point where changes become expensive.

Compare scope, not just totals

A low bid may omit demolition disposal, permits, painting or finish allowances. Put every proposal into a common checklist and identify exclusions. Ask each contractor to price the same drawings and specifications.

Before requesting prices, ask the contractor to explain how this part of the work will be assessed and what assumptions the estimate makes. Request photographs or a sketch when an important condition will be hidden after installation. A clear record helps distinguish an agreed scope from an unforeseen condition.

Understand allowances

An allowance is a placeholder, not necessarily the final cost of tile, fixtures or cabinetry. Confirm whether it includes tax, delivery and installation. Compare allowances against products you would actually choose.

Compare at least two realistic approaches using the same criteria: installation requirements, durability, maintenance and impact on adjacent work. A lower material price may not mean a lower installed cost. Ask which decisions must be finalized before products can be ordered.

Clarify schedule and responsibility

Ask who manages subcontractors, obtains approvals and coordinates inspections. Look for milestones and a process for communicating delays. Confirm whether the owner must supply materials or site access.

Walk through the proposed layout at full scale if possible. Tape dimensions on the floor or wall, check the position of existing services and test everyday movement. This simple exercise often reveals conflicts that are difficult to notice in a small drawing.

Read payment and change-order terms

Payment timing should relate to agreed contract terms and progress, not vague assurances. Require written approval of scope and price changes before extra work proceeds. Understand warranty provisions and dispute procedures.

Confirm the sequence with the people doing the work. One trade may need to complete concealed preparation before another can install visible finishes. Record who supplies each item and who checks dimensions, because missing handoffs can cause delays and rework.

Check qualifications and references

Request appropriate insurance details, relevant project examples and recent references. Verify trade credentials where applicable. A clear, detailed proposal is often a better starting point than a one-page estimate.

At the final walkthrough, compare the completed work with the approved scope and product specifications. Ask for operating instructions, maintenance guidance and warranty documents where applicable. Photograph finished details and retain invoices and approvals for future reference.

Questions to ask before work begins

  • Which existing conditions must be inspected before we commit to the how to compare renovation proposals scope?
  • Which selections and dimensions must be confirmed before ordering?
  • What work is excluded from the estimate, and how will changes be approved?
  • Are any municipal permits, inspections or licensed trades required for this specific scope?
  • How will completed work be inspected and documented?
